Where the models meet

Coverage by domain

The two standards agree almost completely about organizations, enrolment, programs, and staff — the territory CEDS built its Membership spine for. They diverge hardest where Ed-Fi carries operational machinery that CEDS was never designed to hold.

The three real gaps. Survey maps to nothing at all: CEDS 14 carries no survey model, only a single named instrument. Finance maps badly because Ed-Fi ships a full dimensional chart of accounts and CEDS models finance as a handful of account and accountability entities. Intervention has no CEDS counterpart either. Together those three domains account for 24 of the 30 unmapped entities.

None of this is a defect in either standard. It is the practical shape of what a translation layer has to handle: about three quarters of Ed-Fi lands cleanly in CEDS, and the remaining quarter needs either a local extension or a decision that it does not travel.

Why the shapes differ

One spine, two constructions

The single most useful thing to understand before reading the table: CEDS has no Student, Staff, or Parent entity. It has Person, and a person becomes a student by holding an Enrollment — one of eight specialisations of Membership. Ed-Fi does the reverse: it names the people and then makes each specific association a first-class entity.

This is why Student, Staff, and Parent all map to Person as broad matches rather than exact ones, and why the Ed-Fi associations are the rows that hit the CEDS spine dead on. An ingestion mapping should follow the associations, not the entities.
