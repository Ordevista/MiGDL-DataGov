What MiGDL keeps
from Ed-Fi.

Every one of the 81 Ed-Fi resources this pipeline ingests, matched to the MiGDL table that holds it and followed downstream to see how far it actually travels. Both sides are read from the repository — the resource list from configs/endpoints.json, the tables and lineage from the ETL SQL. The rows that stop at Gold are as informative as the rows that reach a data product.

How far each resource travels

Coverage by Ed-Fi domain

Each bar is one Ed-Fi 4.0 domain, split by how completely MiGDL realizes the resources in it. Solid segments reach a Platinum data product. The hatched segment is the striking one: those resources are ingested, flattened, conformed — and then read by nothing downstream.

Half the pipeline stops at Gold. 42 of 81 resources have no derived table or Platinum product reading them anywhere in the ETL. The concentration is not random: 17 of the 18 Michigan extension resources dead-end, almost all of them the Education Entity Master, and Student Cohort, Intervention, and most of Teaching and Learning and School Calendar stop there too.

This is not automatically waste. MiGDL exposes Gold dimensional data through its APIs, so a table with no ETL consumer may still be served directly or queried by analysts. What the finding does establish is that the cost of conforming these resources is being paid every night for value realised outside the pipeline — which is worth confirming deliberately rather than by accident, especially against a twelve-hour window.

Why the halves differ

Two fates for
an Ed-Fi resource

Every resource takes the same path as far as Gold conformed. What separates the two halves of this crosswalk is whether anything is waiting on the other side.

The split is measured by walking the table-level lineage graph forward from each Gold conformed table. A resource counts as reaching a product if any gold_derived or platinum table transitively reads it. Reaching only a staging temp table does not count.
